# OMF2115 — Ensemble story treatment

**Setting:** WAR's 2097 Ganymede tournament
**Status:** Original story proposal for a 3D adaptation, revised against the [original-game lore](OMF2097/OMF2097_LORE.md) and [manual findings](OMF2097/MANUAL_FINDINGS.md). New motives, characters, the deep-link, and branching outcomes below are OMF2115 inventions, not original-game canon.

## 0. Canon boundary and route rule

The original manual establishes ten pilots with separate biographies, remote HAR
combat, the Ganymede representative contest, and a secret Nova project. It does
not establish the deep-link injury conspiracy, a specific Nova consciousness
plan, the new media staff, or the motives added here. Tournament Mode's later
career timeline remains separate from this 2097 story.

Each pilot has a **default path** that unfolds when the player chooses someone
else. Choosing that pilot grants control over their decisions, allies, methods,
and ending. A default betrayal, collapse, romance, death, or discovery is never
guaranteed when that character is the player. No playable pilot is the sole key
to a revelation or resolution; the role can pass to another pilot, a secondary
character, or evidence obtained directly by the player. The player may
challenge, sabotage, spy on, share secrets with, and pursue consensual romance
with eligible adults of any gender. All characters are presumed bisexual;
their default partners do not limit player choice. Romantic routes are offered
only to consenting adults.

Angel's age is unknown in the original manual. OMF2115 establishes her as an
adult, age 28, so she can take part in adult relationships. Steffan remains 17
as in the manual. He can form close friendships and rivalries, but has no
romance or sexual route during the 2097 story.

## 1. Premise

In 2097 WAR turns its search for a Ganymede representative into a televised HAR tournament. Ten pilots, a mix of employees and outsiders, fight through giant machines controlled by neural link. The public sees a glamorous contest whose pilots cannot be struck by the machines' blows.

Behind the cameras, WAR quietly runs a faster **deep-link** on its best performers. It cuts the delay between intention and movement by stimulating the pilot's nervous system, and it records a detailed response map of every session. Those maps are the missing training data for **Nova**, a program to keep a human brain alive inside a robotic body. Major Hans Kreissack, 103, wants a controllable force that can hold Ganymede and make WAR independent of Earth's governments.

Fights leave no wounds. Repeated sessions bring tremors, exhaustion, phantom pain, sleep loss, memory gaps and a quieter damage to how the pilot feels inside their own body. The first rule in every locker room is unspoken: nobody admits it.

**The conspiracy is a chain of compromises, not a single cabal.** Earth's charter officials know the medical reports were altered and tolerate it because WAR's ships and stations keep the colonies alive. Exposing Nova may stop it. It may also stop the supply missions and strand ordinary people.

## 2. Design pillars

1. **Strength is a performance.** Every pilot sells an image, and the image pays their rent.
2. **The prize is real and the price is hidden.** Ganymede offers power, money, access to the Core, and a chance to change lives. Pilots are not fools for wanting it.
3. **Everyone is lying to someone, usually for a good reason.**
4. **Intimacy and surveillance share one corridor.** Every private moment is also a data point to someone.
5. **Spiritual lives, material mystery.** Characters may hold spiritual beliefs and seek meaning through them; the link and Nova still have technological causes.

## 3. Evangelion DNA, translated

| Inspiration | OMF2115 equivalent |
| --- | --- |
| A secretive organization with an agenda behind the "defense" mission | WAR and Nova behind the tournament |
| Pilots chosen by fit; the body as an instrument | Tier-2 enrollment by **Coupling Index** (CI) |
| A synchronization number that measures worth | CI shown on broadcasts as the "Hype Metric"; pilots chase it and fear it |
| Damaged young people shaped by absent or controlling parents | The Devroe parents; Steffan's father; Kreissack as a father figure to the people he uses |
| The hedgehog's dilemma: closeness that wounds | Intimacy between pilots who are also each other's rivals, targets and informants |
| A committee above the commander | The WAR board and the Ganymede charter council |
| Quiet episodes between sorties | Recovery rooms, late-night corridors, canteen scenes |
| Breakdowns that interrupt the plot | Collapse sequences, one per pilot |
| A finale that is personal, not cosmic | Nova is one old man's fear, made industrial |

**Explanation rule:** Nova, symptoms, and uncanny events have material causes. Cossette's spiritual outlook is a genuine personal motivation, not a supernatural explanation for the plot.

## 4. The deep-link, in detail

### 4.1 Tiers

| Tier | Who | What it is | Public framing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tier-1** | Everyone from Round 1 | The standard link. Safe in the way the manual says. | "Remote combat, zero risk." |
| **Tier-2** | Pilots whose CI clears a threshold, offered after Round 1 | Deep-link: lower latency, nervous-system stimulation, full response mapping. | "Elite Link: you earned it." Comes with a bonus and a sponsor clause. |
| **Tier-3** | A handful of Nova candidates | Extended sessions with deeper stimulation and recovery under supervision. | Never mentioned. |

Enrollment is framed as a **reward**. Declining Tier-2 means forfeiting the bonus and the sponsor slot, so refusing looks like weakness.

### 4.2 What it does

- Cuts the latency between intention and machine motion, so a Tier-2 pilot fights faster and cleaner.
- Maps the pilot's nervous response at fine resolution. Each session is training data for Nova.
- Gradually trains the pilot's body to treat the HAR as the "real" body and its own as a lesser input.

### 4.3 The symptom ladder

| Stage | Physical | Psychological |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **I** | Hand tremor, fatigue after matches, tingling | Heightened confidence; mild euphoria after linking |
| **II** | Phantom pain in the limb the HAR was hit on; sleep loss; appetite shifts | Craving for the link; irritability when unlinked; the HAR body feels more "right" |
| **III** | Motor slips (missed clasps, dropped cups); brief memory gaps; chronic pain | Depersonalization in the ordinary body; emotional flattening; time slips |
| **IV** | Seizure-like episodes; lasting motor loss; sustained gaps | Fragmented identity; dependence on linking to feel whole |

The **craving** at Stage II is the story's engine. It means some pilots hide symptoms to *keep* the link, not only to keep their place.

### 4.4 Why pilots conceal

1. **Contracts:** medical withdrawal forfeits purse and sponsor money, and the family gets nothing.
2. **The Evaluation trap:** reporting symptoms triggers a "Supplemental Evaluation" in Dr. Varga's ward, which is where the deepest mapping happens.
3. **Brand:** each pilot sells strength. A shaking hand is a headline.
4. **Rivalry:** weakness shown to an opponent is a weapon handed over.
5. **Craving:** admitting the problem means being taken off the thing that feels like power.

### 4.5 Concealment toolkit

Used by different pilots in different ways:

- Gloves, taped wrists, sleeves, long cuffs.
- Stimulants and quiet private doctors.
- Scripted humor and a ready "nerves" excuse.
- Swapped sleep schedules and fake early nights.
- Deleting or substituting their own telemetry (hacker pilots).
- Letting a rival *think* they are the injured one.

## 5. The machine behind the show

- **WAR board:** needs the tournament to sell HARs and secure Ganymede. Some directors know about Nova; others signed approvals they chose not to read.
- **Major Hans Kreissack, 103:** Nova's driving force; see T7.
- **Dr. Ilse Varga (new, about 41):** chief medical officer of the link program. Brilliant, persuasive, and sincerely convinced that deeper linking can end disability and pain. She runs Supplemental Evaluation and the response maps. Her best argument is also her blind spot: she counts the pilots' consent as already given.
- **Commissioner Adaeze Okonkwo-Hale (new):** the lead charter official. She approved the altered reports because the colonies cannot afford to lose WAR. She is also being blackmailed by a rival consortium.
- **Halvorsen-Tse Orbital (new):** a rival consortium. It wants the link specification and the Ganymede concession. Jean-Paul's handler works for it.
- **Tobias Renn (new):** broadcast director and Shirro's protégé. He cuts the feed when told to and edits storylines for ratings.
- **Nadia Solis (new, 42):** the female studio anchor who presents the newscasts and the contest's public narrative. Her default path begins as a disciplined WAR employee; discrepancies in live footage make her decide whether to protect the network or air evidence. She can challenge an interviewee, shelter a source, or be romanced by an adult player. If she is unavailable, either field reporter or a direct public upload can carry the broadcast reveal.
- **Darius Kade (new, 34):** a male field reporter who follows equipment transfers and arena incidents. His default path starts as an ambitious scoop hunter and ends with a choice between an exclusive and protecting witnesses. He can investigate, trade information, be misled, or be romanced by an adult player. His findings also exist in transport logs and witness testimony.
- **Leena Sato (new, 31):** a female field reporter assigned to the pilots and recovery ward. Her default path is to gather first-person accounts while resisting Renn's pressure to sensationalize them. She can expose altered medical records, refuse a leak, or be romanced by an adult player. Medical staff and patient copies provide alternate evidence if she is absent.
- **Plug McEllis:** the pilots' prep technician from v1. He sees every pilot's hands before every match.
- **Iron Fist, led by Dagny Holt (new):** a violent fixer network tied to the Devroe murders. It can testify, threaten or bargain, but its help cannot erase what it did.

**Money and power flows:** WAR sells HARs on the strength of the broadcast. The Ganymede concession is worth far more than any purse. Halvorsen-Tse wants it. The board wants to sell the link data. Okonkwo-Hale needs the supply contracts to survive politically. The pilots' purses are small by comparison, which is why many of them take the other money too.

## 6. The ensemble

The following arcs are **defaults for an unselected pilot**, not forced outcomes.
The selected pilot may reject the stated want, confess a secret early, ally with
a rival, or choose another method. Each route keeps that pilot's established
background while allowing different loyalties and endings.

### Crystal Devroe, 23 — The Seeker
- **Public mask:** composed, camera-ready, the one who "handles pressure."
- **Want:** proof of what happened to her parents; access to the Core through Ganymede.
- **Wound:** the shuttle, and the memory of being told "an accident" before anyone checked.
- **Secret:** she has already tried the "Nova" access code once, and it raised a flag. WAR knows what she is after.
- **Symptoms and concealment:** tremor in her hands, hidden behind poise and a rehearsed "nerves" line. She is better at hiding it than she is at asking for help.
- **Arc:** from "proof first, consequences later" to accepting that releasing evidence she cannot fully verify can hurt the very people it names.
- **Transformation:** from solitary investigator to someone who shares authority over the evidence.
- **Endings:** she publishes; she steps into oversight; she takes the Ganymede mandate and finds out what the mandate does to her.

### Christian Devroe, 23 — The Shield
- **Public mask:** aggression and confidence.
- **Want:** revenge, and Crystal alive. In his default path, anger draws him toward a violent strike on a WAR facility that he believes will end Nova even if bystanders could be harmed. This is his **terrorist motivation**, framed as a dangerous moral choice rather than a necessary act of heroism.
- **Wound:** having suspected murder for five years and having told no one.
- **Secret:** he has been paying a fixer connected to Iron Fist for information, and in doing so he leaked pieces of Crystal's schedule without understanding where it would go.
- **Symptoms and concealment:** stimulants and bravado; he treats pain as proof he is winning. Varga tells him his symptoms mean he is "exceptional."
- **Arc:** from protecting Crystal by controlling her to trusting her and rejecting violence against civilians. If selected, he may seek lawful exposure, sabotage equipment without harming people, or continue the violent path and face its consequences.
- **Transformation:** from controlled to controlling to released.
- **Endings:** he breaks with Varga and testifies; he stays in her orbit and becomes a Tier-3 candidate; he dies shielding Crystal in the finale (a possible path, not a default).

### Raven, 26 — The Contractor *(brutal mercenary)*
- **Public mask:** Kreissack's bodyguard, silent and feared.
- **Want:** the Ganymede post for his own plans and a way to remove Kreissack, as the manual suggests. In OMF2115 he also seeks a buyout of his contract and enough money never to be owned again.
- **Wound:** years of working for people who treated him as a tool.
- **Secret:** on his default path, he helped cover up the Devroe shuttle operation; records and witnesses can expose it without his cooperation.
- **Symptoms and concealment:** the deep-link gives him sensation he had lost, so he hides the *craving*, not the pain. He tapes his knife hand to hide a tremor.
- **Brutality:** he fights to win with maximum damage. His HAR-damage tactics produce feedback that visibly hurts opponents' pilots, which is the first time the "pilots can't be hurt" line fails in public.
- **Arc:** from "everything has a price" to a choice nobody pays him for.
- **Transformation:** from transaction to refusal.
- **Endings:** he sells the Devroe ledger and lives; he gives it away free and disappears; he dies protecting the evidence he could have sold.

### Jean-Paul, 27 — The Envoy *(spy)*
- **Public mask:** charming junior executive and the social center of the locker room.
- **Want:** to steal the link specification for Halvorsen-Tse and collect his payout. He also reports to an Earth oversight analyst, so he is a double agent whose two masters want different things.
- **Wound:** he grew up watching his father's business collapse after a contract was quietly rewritten; he learned that charm is currency and trust is a mistake. He seeks romantic conquest as proof he cannot be made powerless again.
- **Secret:** he has lost track of which employer he really serves, and he has started to want out.
- **Symptoms and concealment:** numbness in his hands during close contact, hidden behind performance. He uses flirtation and favors as cover.
- **Method:** his public reputation as a **womanizer** helps him approach women with access to stories or records; his actual attraction is not gender-limited. He can use flirtation as an information tactic, or the player can have him abandon that tactic and build an honest relationship.
- **Arc:** from information as intimacy to intimacy as risk.
- **Transformation:** he betrays both masters, and it costs him.
- **Endings:** he becomes the leak that makes the rupture public; he is caught by Halvorsen-Tse; he stays with Milano against both employers' wishes.

### Angel — The Ghost *(hacker)*
- **Age:** 28 in OMF2115; the original manual leaves her age unknown. She is an eligible adult romance option, with her trust and privacy treated as choices rather than rewards for discovering her past.
- **Public mask:** the finalist with no public history.
- **Want:** to restore the erased records of people harmed in earlier link trials, including someone she loves who is in long-term care.
- **Wound:** she saw the first altered telemetry and was erased from the system for it.
- **Secret:** she forged her own entry slot. A later reveal shows she was herself in an earlier trial group, which explains her memory gaps and why she hides.
- **Symptoms and concealment:** insomnia, paranoia, gaps. She hides them with silence, and she substitutes her own telemetry when she can.
- **Tools:** she taps rigs and records other pilots' latency data. She can see who is lying about their coupling numbers. This is a signal artifact, not a mystical sense.
- **Arc:** from invisible to witness.
- **Transformation:** she has to attach her real name to the evidence.
- **Endings:** she releases the archive under her own name; she is traced and silenced; she trades the archive for her loved one's care.

### Ibrahim, 48 — The Builder *(wants to live as a HAR)*
- **Public mask:** the engineer who loves his machines.
- **Want:** to stay alive. He has a progressive neuromuscular condition he has told no one about, and he wants to live inside a HAR rather than die in his body.
- **Wound:** a body that is failing and a lifetime of preferring machines to people.
- **Secret:** he has modified his own rig past Tier-2, so his coupling results are the best in the field. Varga has noticed and is courting him with a Nova-host slot.
- **Symptoms and concealment:** his illness and the link's effects overlap, so he can hide one behind the other.
- **Conflict:** he can spot the deep-link modification before most doctors, and reporting it would end his own path to permanence.
- **Cossette's challenge:** Cossette points out the difference between wanting a machine that extends reach and wanting to flee a body. The story does not treat his wish as foolish and does not treat the body as a defect.
- **Arc:** from wanting to disappear into the machine to deciding what a life in one would cost others.
- **Transformation:** from builder to witness.
- **Endings:** he enters a HAR on his own terms and becomes the voice from inside; he dies teaching a safe link design; he sells everyone out for the Nova slot and is erased.

### Cossette, 39 — The Conscience *(and the flaw in being one)*
- **Public mask:** the designer and former Arena fighter whose story WAR uses as proof that intense linking is safe.
- **Manual anchor:** the Arena accident left her paralyzed from the waist down; her continued design work and relationships do not depend on being cured.
- **Want:** honest consent, and to be seen as more than an inspiring story. Her spiritual practice teaches that a person's body and memories deserve care; she wants to keep the link from reducing people to useful data. This **spiritual motivation** informs her ethics without making the technology mystical.
- **Wound:** being turned into a symbol.
- **Secret:** on her default path, she discovers her old neural recordings were used for the deep-link without informed consent. She has also guessed Ibrahim's condition and kept quiet.
- **Symptoms and concealment:** she recognizes the symptoms first, because the deep-link feels like a harsher version of what she remembers.
- **Flaw:** she needs to be the person who knows better, and she lies by omission to protect people.
- **Arc:** from the quiet authority to someone who has to be publicly wrong.
- **Transformation:** she organizes the pilots into a refusal bloc, and the bloc fractures.
- **Endings:** the bloc holds; it breaks and she takes the blame; she and Ibrahim design a safe link in the aftermath.

### Milano Angston, 35 — The Heir
- **Public mask:** composed, wealthy and unflappable.
- **Want:** WAR back for its original purpose, and a legacy that is his own.
- **Wound:** being the founder's son, always measured against a man who is gone.
- **Secret:** his coup is partly financed by Halvorsen-Tse. He is already half-sold to the people he says he is protecting WAR from.
- **Symptoms and concealment:** private doctors and a manufactured calm. His wealth lets him hide symptoms better than anyone.
- **Arc:** from heir to steward.
- **Transformation:** he surrenders some control, or he becomes another Kreissack.
- **Endings:** he accepts independent oversight that limits him; he takes control and becomes the tyrant he opposed; he and Jean-Paul build something neither planned.

### Shirro, 73 — The Showman
- **Public mask:** the man who invented the tournament, the voice of its glamour.
- **Want:** to be watched; to die famous, not forgotten.
- **Wound:** the fear of irrelevance.
- **Secret:** he approved the policy of cutting away when a pilot falters. He edited the truth long before the story starts.
- **Psychological warfare:** he and Renn pair pilots into "rivalries," stage storylines, and leak personal details for ratings.
- **Arc:** from editor to witness.
- **Transformation:** he uses the broadcast he built to reveal what it hid.
- **Endings:** he keeps the cameras on; he cuts the feed and keeps his seat; he is removed and the cameras keep running without him.

### Steffan Thomas, 17 — The Prodigy
- **No romantic or sexual route during the 2097 story.** No adult character sexualizes him. Player approaches can build mentorship, friendship, rivalry, or shared secrets.
- **Public mask:** arrogant and gifted.
- **Want:** Ganymede as the first step to power, and to be treated as an adult.
- **Wound:** a father on the board who praises his talent and hides the cost.
- **Secret:** he sleeps with the light on and has told no one.
- **Symptoms and concealment:** bravado and jokes; he is the first to collapse publicly (see Act IV).
- **Arc:** from wanting power to wanting a witness.
- **Transformation:** he declines the Tier-3 upgrade, the first act of power that is not winning.
- **Endings:** the adults who protect him do so *with* him, not for him; or they decide for him and he never forgives it.

### Major Hans Kreissack, 103 — The Commander
- **Public mask:** the old chief, and the owner of the tournament's final fight.
- **Want:** to survive and to keep control.
- **Fear:** that his age will make him dispensable.
- **Argument:** the pilots' suffering is a prototype flaw to optimize away, and the colonies need security. The second half is true.
- **Arc:** see T7 below.

## 7. Player-directed relationships and actions

The pairings below are **default NPC developments** only. When a pilot is
selected, the player may approach any consenting adult pilot or eligible
secondary character, regardless of gender. All characters are presumed
bisexual; only adults have romance routes during this story. Romance is
optional, can be declined or ended, and
never unlocks the only copy of critical evidence. Intimate scenes stay
suggestive and skippable. Steffan's routes remain nonromantic while he is 17.

| Thread | Dynamic | What it complicates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Crystal and Raven** | Attraction between a seeker and the man who helped bury her answer | His secret (T5). Every intimate scene is poisoned by what he has not said. |
| **Jean-Paul and Milano** | Two operators seducing each other for leverage, with real feeling arriving uninvited | Milano's covert financing; Jean-Paul's two handlers |
| **Cossette and Ibrahim** | A slow, tender bond between two adults who understand what machines mean | Ibrahim's hidden illness and his Nova ambition |
| **Christian and Dr. Varga** | A fraught default attraction across an abuse of power; the player may reject her influence | His trust in her; Crystal's trust in him |
| **Nadia, Darius, and Leena** | Three independent adult romance routes; each may also remain a professional ally or adversary | Broadcast access, source protection, and what the public learns |
| **Shirro and the audience** | A one-sided love affair with being watched | The feed |

**Manipulation as a choice:** Jean-Paul and Varga may use attraction for leverage on their default paths, but a selected pilot can refuse or expose the tactic. Romance is never required to spy or obtain documents.

**Sample beat, Raven and Crystal:** a late-night corridor. Raven offers a stolen medical drive instead of an excuse. Crystal may question him, take the drive, share a lead, pursue a kiss, or walk away. If neither is the player, their default attraction may develop. The same records can be found through Leena, a medical copy, or a player-led intrusion.

### Between-match choice vocabulary

| Action | Immediate opportunity | Possible consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Challenge | Call out a rival, official, or reporter in private or on air. | A public answer, a duel, a hardened rivalry, or a witness. |
| Sabotage | Interfere with a broadcast, robot, or archive route. | Delayed plans, suspicion, investigation, or harm that the story acknowledges. |
| Spy | Follow a person, inspect access logs, or plant a listening device. | Evidence and leverage, with trust or exposure costs. |
| Share secrets | Tell an ally or journalist part or all of what was learned. | Shared protection, leaks, changed loyalties, or a route to publication. |
| Romance | Approach a consenting adult and build a relationship over scenes. | Intimacy and perspective, without making evidence or victory dependent on it. |

Every pilot can use these approaches. Background and relationships affect the
response, but no pilot is forced into one moral role. A selected Christian can
reject terrorism; a selected Jean-Paul can stop treating people as conquests;
a selected Cossette can question her spiritual practice. An NPC may still
follow their default path unless player actions change it.

### Replaceable plot functions

| Plot function | Default carrier | Other routes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find Nova records | Crystal or Angel | Jean-Paul's access, Darius's transfers, Leena's sources, direct Core intrusion |
| Prove medical tampering | Cossette or Leena | Varga's staff, pilot telemetry, recovered reports, a player confession |
| Reveal shuttle evidence | Christian or Raven | Iron Fist witness, archive copy, Darius's investigation |
| Air the truth | Shirro or Nadia | Either reporter, hijacked feed, independent public release |
| Keep Ganymede supplied | Milano | Charter officials, alternate contractors, a player-negotiated mandate |
| Confront Kreissack and Nova | Tournament winner | Whoever the player chooses to play; allies can assist without replacing the selected lead |

The finale checks whether the needed **facts and public support** exist, not
whether a named pilot survived, cooperated, or was romanced. If an NPC is
defeated, refuses, disappears, or changes sides, another route remains open.

## 8. Psychological warfare catalogue

| Tactic | Used by | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leaking a rival's medical data to the press | Shirro/Renn, Jean-Paul | Forces concealment, breeds paranoia |
| Staged rivalries and romances for ratings | Shirro, Renn | Pilots start acting the script |
| Offering Tier-2 as a prize | Varga, WAR | Turns the trap into an incentive |
| Pre-fight provocation | Raven, Jean-Paul, Milano | Mind games that shape morale in-story |
| Threatening families or dependents | Iron Fist, Raven | Pressure to withdraw or stay silent |
| Telling a pilot their symptoms prove they are special | Varga | Makes the damage a source of pride |
| Fabricated "evidence" planted for a sibling | Christian's fixer, Iron Fist | Splits the Devroes |
| Using consent forms against the signer | WAR legal | Turns a signature into a cage |
| Selective kindness | Cossette, Jean-Paul | Builds trust that can be spent later |

**Presentation rule (from v1):** any mind-game effect on gameplay is visible and opt-in. Narrative choices never secretly degrade fighting controls.

## 9. Structure

**Format.** Qualifier league of three rounds (15 matches), top four to the semifinals, two to the final, and the winner faces Kreissack in the **Representative's Duel**. Tier-2 offers go out after Round 1. This bracket is an OMF2115 proposal, not a rule specified by the original manual.

| Act | Focus | Key beats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Prologue: Opening Ceremony** | Ensemble | Ten pilots, ten masks. Broadcast glamour; the first tremor goes unremarked. |
| **I. The Showcase** | Round 1 | The "safe" spectacle. Each pilot's wants and masks established. Tier-2 offers. |
| **II. The Prize** | Round 2 | Pilots accept or decline Tier-2. First symptoms. Varga begins courting. Jean-Paul starts his work. |
| **III. Fractures** | Round 3 | Rivalries become real. Raven's brutality crosses the safety line. Twists T3, T4. Angel's taps surface contradictions. |
| **IV. The Collapse** | Semifinals | On the default path Steffan collapses live; if his route diverges, another pilot's evidence or symptoms expose the deep-link. Shirro, Nadia, or the player decides whether the feed stays live. |
| **V. Nova** | Finals | The board's betrayal of Kreissack (T7). The final duel. Intercut with allies saving files. |
| **Epilogue: After the Arena** | Outcomes | Nova's lab, Core access, and the choice of what to publish respond to player action. |

**Episode rhythm.** Each chapter foregrounds one pilot, while the others are visible in the corners of the frame. Every pilot receives a pressure point, a secret that can be uncovered or withheld, and a choice with a cost. Breakdown, confession, and betrayal are possible outcomes, not mandatory scenes.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Crystal | Investigates alone, then shares the Nova evidence. | Bargain with WAR, expose it early, or trust a different ally. |

| Jean-Paul | Uses romance and espionage, then betrays both handlers. | Work for one side, become an honest partner, or publish his files. |

| Milano | Seeks to reclaim WAR while hiding dubious financing. | Disclose the funding, reject the takeover, or build another coalition. |
| Shirro | Edits the spectacle, then keeps the feed live. | Cut the feed, step aside, or expose WAR at another time. |
| Steffan | Chases status, collapses, then declines the upgrade. | Ask for help earlier, resist WAR publicly, or pursue the upgrade with informed consequences. |

The selected pilot's actions and relationships determine their path; the
table never overrides a player decision. Kreissack is an antagonist, not a
playable route in this treatment.

## 12. Endings

Shared state variables: **Archive** (how much evidence survives), **Trust** (who the player allied with), **Cost** (accumulated link damage), **Witness** (how public the truth became).

| Outcome | Gain | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Publish everything** | The deep-link trial and Nova contracts become public; pilots and families can demand an accounting. | Supply and security contracts freeze while Ganymede is vulnerable. Allies' compromises are exposed too. |
| **Rebuild WAR under oversight** | Milano, another WAR faction, or a charter coalition keeps stations supplied; trials stop; an independent board gains access. | Reform rests on institutions that looked away. Evidence can be buried again without real enforcement. |
| **Take Ganymede's mandate** | The winner protects colonists and secures records before releasing them. | Temporary secrecy resembles WAR's old justification, and power changes the person who holds it. |
| **Quiet settlement** *(new, bad ending)* | Pilots receive compensation and the Ganymede charter is signed. | Nothing is published and Nova continues elsewhere. |

Every pilot also has an individual epilogue shaped by the player's actions;
the possibilities in §6 are examples, not fixed endings. The outcomes above
set the world, while pilot and media-character epilogues describe the people.

## 13. Tone and presentation

- Keep the original's colorful HAR silhouettes, exaggerated combat, arena hazards and sharp pre-fight banter.
- Show public glamour beside private recovery: press lights, tailored looks, locker-room warmth, trembling hands, late-night calls.
- Make fights feel draining through story, sound, animation and recovery choices. Never degrade controls secretly.
- Keep the danger political, medical and emotional. No cosmic beings, no mystical explanations for Nova.
- Let antagonists argue coherently. Kreissack is wrong about owning human lives. The need for safe space travel and colony security is real.
- Any fanservice stays light, character-led and limited to established adults. It never replaces consent or a character's own goals.
- It must never imply that disability needs to be cured or that a life outside a HAR is lesser.

## 14. Sample scene: the green room

*After Round 2. The broadcast green room is nearly empty. Milano pours water with a hand that does not shake. Jean-Paul leans in the doorway, jacket open, watching it.*

**Jean-Paul:** You've stopped shaking.

**Milano:** I never started.

**Jean-Paul:** Everyone started. Some people just pay to stop it.

*Milano sets the glass down. He does not look at him.*

**Milano:** That sounds like an accusation.

**Jean-Paul:** It sounds like a compliment. I'd like to know who your doctor is.

*Jean-Paul crosses the room and straightens Milano's collar. His fingers linger half a second too long. Milano lets him.*

**Milano:** You're very good at this.

**Jean-Paul:** At what?

**Milano:** Making me forget what you want.

*Jean-Paul smiles. His hand, resting against Milano's shoulder, has gone numb. He does not mention it.*

*Offscreen, a technician is rewriting a log. On the wall monitor, the CI leaderboard updates: both of their numbers have gone up.*

The scene makes four things playable at once: concealed symptoms, espionage, mutual seduction, and a data trail. The player can press Jean-Paul, warn Milano, steal the log, or leave.

## 15. First slice to build

Do **not** begin with a single sibling match. Build a **two-match ensemble slice**:

1. A Round 1 match with the broadcast spectacle.
2. The Tier-2 offer scene, with at least three pilots reacting differently.
3. One concealment moment (a glove, a missed clasp, a "nerves" line).
4. The green room scene above.

It shows the cast, the tone, the trap and the core question: *what will you hide to stay strong?*
